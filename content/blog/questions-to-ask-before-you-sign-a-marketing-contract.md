The best time to ask a difficult question is before you sign. After you sign, the same question sounds like a complaint, and it gets answered like one.

None of the questions below is clever. That is the point. A vendor who answers them in writing is easier to work with than one who answers them warmly on a call.

## Start with the month, not the pitch

Ask what will exist at the end of month one that does not exist now, who makes it, and when you will see it. If the answer is a feeling rather than a list, keep asking.

## Deliverables

- A written list of what is included, with quantities and dates.
- What happens when something on that list is late.
- Who does the work, and whether any of it is done by someone you have never met.
- What is explicitly **not** included. This list is usually longer than the other one and more useful.

## Accounts and ownership

- Who owns the domain, the website, the business listing, the analytics, the ad account and the social profiles.
- Are those accounts created under your business, or under the vendor's?
- Can you get access today — and can you keep that access if you leave?
- Who holds the passwords? A vendor should not need your password through a form or a chat message. They should be given their own access, and you should be able to remove it.

## Your data when you leave

- What happens to data collected on your behalf when the contract ends: export, retention, deletion, and in what timeframe.
- Whether you can take a copy with you, and in what format.

Our answer, for the record: connected data is deleted on disconnect, and connecting an account is permission for that connection only — not marketing consent for anything else.

## Term, cancellation and exit

- The length of the term, the notice period, and whether it renews automatically.
- What you pay if you leave early, and whether work already paid for is delivered.
- Whether the price can change mid-term, and on what notice.

## What the vendor does not promise

Ask them to put it in writing: no search position, no ranking, no number of customers, no revenue figure, no outcome they cannot control.

If a vendor will not write that sentence down, you have learned something more useful than any answer they would have given you.

## How the work will be measured

- Which numbers will appear each month.
- Which numbers will be marked unavailable, and what a missing number prints as.
- Whether any figure is an estimate, and how an estimate is labelled.

A report that cannot say "we do not have this" is a report you cannot audit.

## What we do not promise

BravData does not promise a search position, a ranking, a number of customers or a revenue figure. The Visibility Score is our own calculation from public information, not a search position, and it prints the method version that produced it. Where a source cannot be read, we mark it as not checked rather than scoring it as zero. We have no client stories to publish yet, and we will not invent one.

## What this list cannot tell you

- Whether the vendor will do good work. No clause in a contract makes that true.
- Whether the price is fair. We do not publish what other vendors charge, and we are not going to guess at it.
- Whether the contract is right for your situation. Read it, and take advice on it if the amount matters to you.
