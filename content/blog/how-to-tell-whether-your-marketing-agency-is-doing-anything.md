Every month a document arrives. It has a logo on it, a chart that goes up to the right, and a paragraph about momentum. What it rarely has is a list of things that were done to your business, with dates.

Here is a way to read that document, and a way to ask for a better one.

## Three things get confused

There is the **work** — pages written, listings corrected, campaigns built. There is the **change** — what is different about your business this month than last. And there is the **measurement** — the numbers.

A report proves the third, and often not even that. It is evidence of reporting. Nothing more.

## What to ask for each month

1. An itemised list of what was done, with a date against each item and the name of the person or system that did it.
2. Your own access to the thing itself — the domain, the site, the business listing, the analytics. Not a screenshot of it. A login you hold.
3. The source of every number: which tool produced it, which date range it covers, and whether it is measured or estimated.
4. What was decided **not** to do, and why. A vendor with no "no" list is either doing everything or reporting on nothing.
5. Any change to your site or your listing, with the before and after, so you can see it yourself.
6. One named person who is accountable for the month and reachable without a gatekeeper.

## "Doing something" versus "sending reports"

These are the patterns worth noticing. None of them proves bad faith — a vendor can be busy and still produce a report that reads like a weather forecast — but each one is worth a question.

- The same numbers reappear every month with no action attached to them.
- The only metrics are ones that can only go up, such as impressions, reach and followers, and none of them touch anything you control.
- Nothing in the report ever says "we tried this and it did not work".
- Nobody can tell you who did the work, or when.
- The only visible change this month is the invoice.

## The one test worth running

Pick a single item from last month's report — one page edited, one listing field corrected, one post published — and ask to see it live.

If it exists and matches what the report says, the report is describing work. If nobody can show it to you, the report was the work.

## Apply it to us

Our free check produces a score with findings attached, each naming the source and the date it was read, and it labels what it could not check instead of scoring it. That is a reading of public information. It is not a list of work done on your behalf, and it should not be mistaken for one.

In a paid engagement, the work is supposed to appear as tasks, plan items and changes you can see for yourself, and the monthly report is supposed to keep a measured number visibly separate from a missing one.

We are early. We have no client story to show you, and we will not invent one. Apply the checklist to us hardest of all: ask for the itemised list, the sources, and the things we decided not to do.

## What this checklist cannot tell you

- Whether the work caused the change. Nothing in a monthly report can prove that.
- Whether the price is fair. We do not benchmark what vendors charge, and a report is not the place to find that out.
- Whether the numbers are complete. Only the source and the date let you check that.
- Whether the work is any good. That is a judgement about your customers, not a metric.
