"Marketing budget for dental practice", "marketing budget for dental office", "how much does dental marketing cost" — all real phrasings. The usual answer is a share of revenue. We will not give you one; the rest of this is the arithmetic we would use instead, from numbers only you have.

## Why we will not quote a percentage

The standard advice is a range of revenue to spend on marketing. We hold no benchmark. We have not surveyed practices and cannot see what others spend, their books or their contracts. A figure lifted from an article about a different market would not be evidence about yours, and printing it here would make us look like we had data we do not have.

The same goes for a cost per new patient: we could only invent it. We are not your accountant either — what you can afford is your own question, with your own adviser if you want one.

## Start with what a new patient is worth to you

Pick one service you would like more of. From your own billing, write down what a first visit for that service bills at, how often a patient like that comes back over a year, and roughly how long they stay. That gives you a value for one patient of that kind.

We are not printing a number there because the only useful one is yours; any figure we wrote would be invented, and you would be planning against it.

## Then say how many you need

A goal you can state out loud: "four more of these a month." Multiply it by the value you worked out. That is the ceiling on what those extra patients are worth to you, before the cost of treating them. Judge every quote against that number, not against a percentage someone else uses.

## Split the one-time from the monthly

Setup — site, tracking, baseline, plan — is paid once. The monthly covers work that repeats. Ask which is which in writing and get the one-time part itemised line by line. Then ask what is a pass-through: ad spend is not the fee, and the two are often added so a single number looks like the price.

## Ask for hours, and ask who owns things

"How many hours of work, at what rate, doing what?" turns a proposal into something you can compare with another proposal. Then: who owns the website, the listing and the account logins, what notice you must give, and what you keep if you leave. [Questions to ask before you sign a marketing contract](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/questions-to-ask-before-you-sign-a-marketing-contract) is the longer version of that conversation.

## What costs nothing

Correcting the contradiction between your hours, phone and services, replying to reviews, asking for reviews, answering the phone. Those are habit and time, not retainer. [Check your hours, phone and services first](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/hours-phone-and-services-first) is that pass done in an afternoon.

## What this cannot tell you

- Whether a quote is fair for your market. We cannot see other practices' contracts.

- Whether the cheaper option would have worked.
