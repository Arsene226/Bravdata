The BravData Visibility Score is a number from 0 to 100 that describes how findable and how clear your public presence is, by our method, on the date we read it. It is our own calculation. It is not a figure handed over by Google, Meta or an AI vendor, and no one outside those companies can read their internal numbers — so anyone claiming to hand you one is describing something else.

## Seven components, with published weights

The score is built from up to seven components:

- **Website Health — weight 20.** What a page returns when it is read: structure, headings, phone-friendliness, how quickly it answers.
- **Content Authority — weight 20.** Whether your pages actually say what you do, for whom, and where.
- **Search Presence — weight 15.** Whether the public pages that describe you are reachable and current.
- **Local Presence — weight 15.** The local details a customer acts on: hours, location, area served.
- **Reputation — weight 10.** Public review signals, where they can be read.
- **Authority Signals — weight 10.** Whether other public pages point at you.
- **AI Readiness — weight 10.** How clearly your pages answer the plain question of what you do and where.

The weights are owner-confirmed (7 October 2026) and they are printed beside every score we return, so nobody has to guess whose numbers these are. Inside each component, the individual checks carry points that add up to 100 inside that component, and they live in one published registry rather than being typed beside each check. Any change to a weight, a threshold or a check is a change to the method, and the method has a version.

## "Up to seven", and why that matters

If a component cannot be evidenced from public sources, it is not scored zero and it is not estimated. It is left out, and the report says which part is missing and why.

That is the difference between a report and a guess. A missing source printed as zero is a number we invented; a missing source printed as missing is a fact about the check. The same rule applies to the findings: each one names the source we read and the date we read it, so you can go and look yourself.

Where a source becomes readable because you connected an account, it stops printing as not checked and starts printing as an observation. The report names which sources are connected; while they are not, the honest line stays.

## What the score is not

- **Not a search ranking.** Search results cannot be read to a position from outside the search engine, and they differ by person, place and day. We do not print a position.
- **Not a promise of customers.** It describes how findable and how clear your public presence is, and it stops there.
- **Not a benchmark.** We print no "average business" figure, because we have not measured an average business. There is no comparison table hiding behind the number.
- **Not a verdict on your business.** It is a read of the public picture, not of your waiting room.
