"Is paying for Google ads worth it" and "is it worth paying for google ads" are real phrasings people type when they search this. We hold no volume tool, so we cannot tell you how often. We can tell you something more useful: we will not answer it for you, because the answer lives in your booking book.

Here is the procedure instead.

## Why no one can give you a verdict

Anyone selling ads can show you their other clients. We cannot see that practice's market, front desk or books, so their result is not evidence about yours. A figure quoted as typical is someone else's arithmetic, and we will not print one here.

## What you can observe

Four things, until you add tools:

- **Spend.** What left the account per week, including the management fee if separate.
- **Clicks.** What the ad platform reports — its own number, not a patient count.
- **Calls and forms, if tracked.** A call from a click and a call from a search result arrive at the same phone; if nothing separates them, you cannot attribute either.
- **Bookings, if tracked.** The only one that happens in your practice rather than in someone's dashboard.

These live in three systems — the ad platform, your phone or website, your scheduling software — and they will not agree. That is normal, and it is why the next step matters.

## Record a baseline before you spend

Take four weeks where you change nothing. Count new-patient calls and bookings per week, and note how many of each you could trace to a named source. That baseline is the only thing a later month can be compared against.

If the answer is "we do not know where new patients come from," stop here. Ads on top of that are spend you cannot judge, and no report will fix it.

## Run the decision, not just the campaign

Fix the period in advance — not after you have seen the numbers — then ask four questions:

1. Did bookings for the service I advertised go up, against the baseline?
2. Were the calls the right kind of call, or mostly questions the front desk could not convert?
3. What did I spend in total, including my own hours on it?
4. Knowing what one patient of that kind is worth to me, was the first answer worth the third?

That last comparison is yours — the value of a patient in [what practice marketing should cost](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/what-practice-marketing-should-cost) is built from your billing, not ours. We are not giving you a target ratio; one from us would be invented.

## Things that look like answers but are not

A rising click count is not a rising booking count. "Leads" reported by a vendor are often form fills, and a form fill is not a patient. A month when the phone rang more may simply have had more working days in it.

## When the honest answer is "not yet"

Ads are a reasonable thing to try when your listing is correct, your hours and phone are right, and someone answers during the hours you advertise. They are a poor first purchase when the front door is the problem. [Check your hours, phone and services first](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/hours-phone-and-services-first) is an afternoon's work and costs nothing.

## What this cannot tell you

- Whether ads work in your specialty or your part of town. We hold no data on that.
- What you should spend, or what clicks should cost you.
- What return to expect, from any amount.
- Whether your profession's advertising rules allow a claim in your ad. Check the rule that applies to you with your own compliance advisor.
