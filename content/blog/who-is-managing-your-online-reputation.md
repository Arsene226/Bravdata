"Online reputation management for doctors", "for physicians", "for healthcare", "reputation management tool for doctors" — real phrasings people type when they search this. We hold no volume tool, so we cannot say how often. One thing worth noticing before you read the results: nearly every page that answers this is selling the service. That does not make them dishonest, but it does mean you are reading proposals, not advice.

The answer to the headline question is usually duller. Here is how to find yours.

## What "reputation" is made of

Three things, all visible:

- **The profiles you control.** Your listing on the maps app, your website, the directories nobody remembers signing up to.
- **The reviews you can reply to.** What patients wrote, and whether anyone answered.
- **What the first page of your name shows.** Search your practice name and look at what comes back.

That is the whole surface — not a hidden score a vendor can see and you cannot.

## The check, in an afternoon

In a private window, search your practice name and note what appears and whether it is current. Then, on a phone at the practice, search one of your services with "near me" — that is what a patient in the waiting room sees.

Then open each profile you own and check hours, phone, address, categories, services, photos. Wrong hours and a dead number are the two mistakes that cost an owner most, and both are free to fix. [Your Google Business Profile: the owner's list](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/your-google-business-profile-the-owners-list) is the checklist.

Finally, read your last ten reviews and count those with no reply. That count is the clearest signal of whether anyone is managing this at all.

## So who is doing it now?

Ask out loud in the practice. The answer is usually one of four:

- Nobody, since the person who set the listing up left.
- The front desk, when someone remembers.
- The website company, bundled in, unnamed.
- A monthly service nobody can describe in a sentence.

Whichever it is, put a name against it. "Marketing" is not a person, and a responsibility with no name behind it is not being done.

## Four questions for anyone selling you reputation help

1. Which of those three will you change, and where will I see it?
2. What will you do about a review I think is unfair? If the answer involves promising to get it taken down, be careful — removal is the platform's decision, not a vendor's favour.
3. Who owns the profiles and logins, and what do I keep if I stop paying?
4. What is the fee buying — replies, posts, corrections, reports — in hours?

Two answers should end the conversation: writing reviews on your behalf, and generating reviews in bulk from a list. Both put something under your name that your patients did not say.

## The part nobody sells

Replying to every review, asking patients for their honest account, keeping hours and phone correct — time rather than retainer, and it shows in public. [How to ask patients for Google reviews](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/how-to-ask-patients-for-google-reviews) is the habit, and [how to tell whether your marketing agency is doing anything](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/how-to-tell-whether-your-marketing-agency-is-doing-anything) is how to check the rest.

## What this cannot tell you

- Whether your reputation is costing you patients. We cannot see who chose another practice, or why.
- How to have a review removed, or whether one is eligible. That is the platform's process, not ours, and no one should promise you an outcome.
- Whether a vendor's promises can be kept. We do not know their clients' books.
- Where you appear compared with other practices, beyond what you see yourself on the day you look.

A machine can read your public pages and signals and tell you what it found, with dates — [what a free visibility check is and is not](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/what-a-free-visibility-check-is-and-is-not).
