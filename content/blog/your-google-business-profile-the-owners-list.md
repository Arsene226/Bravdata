People type "how to set up a google business profile" to find the thing, and then they type the other question: "how to rank higher on google my business", "how to increase google business ranking". This answers the first and refuses the second; the refusal is the more useful half.

## What the listing is

The panel that answers when someone searches your practice name, or a service plus your town. It holds your hours, phone, address, services, photos, questions and reviews. Part of it you edit directly. Part of it is assembled from what is already public about you, which is why it can be right about your name and wrong about your Saturday hours.

## Hours, phone and address

Check all three logged out, on a phone. Then check the same three on your website and in your booking tool. Two different answers is worse than one wrong answer, because the person reading decides both are unreliable and calls someone else. Holiday hours are where listings drift first.

## Primary category and services

The primary category decides which searches you are even eligible for, and it is the setting most owners set once and never open again. Services should be the words patients use — "teeth cleaning" as well as "prophylaxis" — because the listing is read by people, not by a professional body. Nothing goes on that list you do not actually offer.

## Photos

Photographs of your own front desk, treatment rooms and building help a nervous patient know what they are walking into. Stock images do the opposite: they look like every other clinic and tell a patient nothing. Check the date on the last one you added.

## Questions and answers

Anyone can ask a question on the listing and anyone can answer it — including people who do not work at your practice. Read yours. Answer what only you can answer, briefly and factually, and correct an answer that is wrong wherever the platform lets you.

## Your description

Say plainly what you do, who you treat and where you are, in the words a patient would use for their problem. Leave out anything you cannot support in writing, and put nothing there that your profession's advertising rules would not allow on your own site.

## Reviews

Ask, reply, take the answer. [How to ask patients for Google reviews](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/how-to-ask-patients-for-google-reviews) covers that half of the listing.

## The "rank higher" question

Two engines return real, commonly-typed phrasings about appearing higher, so the question is worth answering. We are not going to hand you a list of factors, because we have not tested how the ordering works, we cannot see how it is decided, and anything we wrote would be a story about a system we do not operate.

Results also differ by person, place, device and day. A factor that seemed to help one practice for one month would be a coincidence dressed up as a method. What we can say is narrower and still worth doing: a listing whose hours are right, whose services are named the way patients say them, and whose questions get answered is not working against the person trying to book you.

## Do this once a quarter

Logged out, on a phone, with the date at the top of your notes. Fix the contradictions first: they are the cheapest items on the list. The wider version of that pass is [how to audit your public presence in 30 minutes](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/how-to-audit-your-public-presence-in-30-minutes).

## What this cannot tell you

- Whether your listing is why someone called.
- Where you appear, or why a competitor appears instead.
- What the ordering does this month. Nobody outside the platform can see it, including us.
- Whether a vendor's optimisation package changes anything you can measure.
