Most owners know their business from the inside: signed into their own accounts, on a desktop, looking at a dashboard that shows their own posts and their own listing. A customer sees almost none of that. They see a phone, a search box, and whatever answers first.

This is a do-it-yourself version of that view. It takes about half an hour, it costs nothing, and it leaves you with dated notes instead of an opinion.

## What you need

- Your phone, on mobile data rather than your office wifi, with location on.
- A browser you are not signed into — private or incognito mode is enough.
- Somewhere to write. Put the date at the top of every entry before you start.

## Step 1: search your business name

Type your business name the way a customer would: the name on your sign, not the name you use internally. Write down:

- Whether you appear at all.
- The form you appear in — your website, a directory, a social page, a map result.
- Which phone number and which opening hours are shown.
- Anything obviously stale: an old address, a closed branch, last year's offer.

You are not reading a ranking out of this. One search, on one phone, in one place, is not a position, and results differ by person, place and day. You are checking whether the basics are right.

## Step 2: search what you do, in your area

Now search the words a customer would use for the work — the service, plus your town or neighbourhood. Do it for three services you actually sell, not one.

Note what comes back and whether you appear. Do not conclude anything yet about why. Record what you saw, and when.

## Step 3: read your listing as a customer

Open your business listing and read it top to bottom as though you were deciding whether to call.

- Hours, including holidays.
- The phone number, and whether it is tappable.
- The services listed, in the words customers use.
- Photos: do they look like the business today?
- The last date anything was posted.
- Whether anyone asked a question and got an answer.

## Step 4: open your website on the phone

- How long it takes before the page is readable.
- Whether the phone number is visible without scrolling.
- Whether the service you are known for is named on the first screen.
- Whether the page says where you are and when you are open.
- Whether there is an obvious way to call, book or message.

## Step 5: compare the three surfaces

Put your listing, your website and your booking page side by side and look for disagreements: different hours, a number that rings nowhere, a service only one of them lists. A customer who sees two different answers usually treats both as unreliable.

Fix the disagreements first. They are usually the cheapest thing on the list and the fastest to do.

## What this check cannot tell you

- Your position in anyone's search results.
- How many people saw you, or how many called.
- Why a competitor appeared and you did not.
- Whether a change you make caused anything. If you want to know whether something moved, take the same notes again later — and even then you are comparing evidence, not proving cause.

Thirty minutes, once a month, with the date written down, is worth more than an hour of guessing.
