This is a description of a business's public website, not a review and not an endorsement. BravData looked at afrozoneflix.com on 2026-11-04 and again on 2026-11-14. We have no business relationship with AfroZoneFlix, no one paid for this post, and we have not seen anything behind its front door — we describe what a visitor sees on its public pages, plus the one announcement the business gave us directly, which is labelled as such and which we have not verified.

## An announcement from AfroZoneFlix, in its own words

AfroZoneFlix gave BravData this announcement directly on 2026-11-14:

> AfroZoneFlix is recruiting brokers/representatives to help find or source movies in Africa.

That is the whole of what we were given, and it is reproduced as the business's own statement, not as a finding of ours. **We have not verified it.** We have not checked whether the recruiting is real, current or open, and we add nothing to it: no pay, no commission, no eligibility, no partnership terms, no deadline and no result. If you want the terms, ask AfroZoneFlix.

The site itself did not carry this announcement when we looked on 2026-11-14 — /brokers and /careers both answered 404, and no page we read mentioned brokers or representatives.

## What a visitor saw on 2026-11-04

On 2026-11-04, requesting afrozoneflix.com sent a visitor to /coming-soon, and that page's own title is "Coming soon — AfroZoneFlix". Its own meta description reads: "AfroZoneFlix is a premium virtual cinema for African film, concerts and comedy. Opening soon."

The wordmark is set as three stacked words — "Afro", "Zone", "Flix" — beside a stylised "A" mark, and the headline under it is "A virtual cinema for African stories".

The same line then appears three times in three languages: "Coming soon", "Bientôt disponible", "Em breve". Underneath it, the page says what the site is for:

> African film, live concerts and comedy — screening in a premium virtual cinema you can watch from anywhere. The doors open soon.

The footer says it again in its own words: "A premium virtual cinema for African film, concerts and comedy — streamed to the diaspora, wherever the diaspora is."

For a visitor without a code there was one thing here to act on: the preview-code block asked "Have a preview code?" and said "Enter it to look around before we open." There is one Enter button, and a note beside it explains what a code is for: "AfroZoneFlix — African cinema, concerts and comedy, in one place. If you were given a code, it opens the whole site on this browser for 30 days."

The navigation already named where the site is headed — "Browse events", "Stories", "My tickets", "Sign in" — and a footer column headed "Watch" lists "All events", "Cinema", "Concert", "Comedy" and "Upcoming directors".

One line across the page reads "Tonight in Lagos · London · Atlanta · Toronto · Nairobi · Accra · Houston", and beside it the site states two of its own rules: "Every showtime is announced in its own timezone. Tickets are bought with a card."

The site's contact block lists a San Antonio, Texas address and an email address. The footer carries the copyright line "© 2026 AfroZoneFlix" and links to its own X and Instagram accounts.

## What changed when we looked again on 2026-11-14

The front door is different now. Requesting afrozoneflix.com redirected to www.afrozoneflix.com and served a home page whose own title is "AfroZoneFlix — African film, concerts and comedy", with the meta description "Scheduled screenings of African film, concerts and comedy — buy a ticket, then watch in the browser."

Under the headline "A virtual cinema for African stories" and the line "Film, concerts and comedy — streamed to the diaspora", the home page says:

> Scheduled screenings from Lagos, London, Nairobi and Atlanta, each with its own showtime and ticket price. The schedule can't be listed on this page right now, so there is nothing to browse and nothing to buy at this moment.

Below that it shows the heading "The schedule is temporarily unavailable" and the line "We couldn't load the screenings just now. Refresh in a moment and try again."

A section headed "One cinema, every timezone" carries the line "Everywhere the diaspora is" and this explanation: "Showtimes are announced in the city's own timezone, so a 7pm screening in Lagos is shown as 6pm in Accra and 9pm in Nairobi. Buy once, watch from wherever you are."

The addresses that once bounced a visitor without a code back to the coming-soon page now serve their own pages: /events, /blog, /privacy and /terms each answered 200. /events has its own title, "Screenings on sale — AfroZoneFlix", and reports "9 events". The three listings we could read in its page text are "Amplify: Rooftop Sessions", "Laugh Riot: Naija Nights" and "Diaspora Drumline Live", each carrying the label "Sample screening", with $37.50, $6.30 and $37.50 beside member prices of $22.50, $3.78 and $22.50 and a "$5.99/month membership". The label is the site's own, so we treat those listings and prices as its sample data rather than as confirmed showings.

/blog has the title "The blog — AfroZoneFlix", the heading "Stories from the cinema" and the line "Notes on African film, concerts and comedy — and the screenings they belong to". It lists five posts, all dated 27 October 2026: "Welcome to the AfroZoneFlix blog", "Peckham Gold joins the schedule", "This week in the cinema", "Why we watch African film together" and "Coming up: what premieres next".

/coming-soon still answers, still with "Coming soon", "Bientôt disponible" and "Em breve" and the same body line. It now also says "You already have access" and "This browser is signed in with a preview code — or the launch gate is off, in which case the site is open to everyone", with one button reading "Enter the site".

## What stands out

Five things here are worth pointing at, and all five are the site's own statements rather than our judgment:

- **The recruiting announcement is the business's own, and it is not on its site.** It reached us directly on 2026-11-14, in the one sentence quoted above, and we have published it as theirs. We did not find it on afrozoneflix.com, and we have verified none of it.
- **The door was genuinely shut on 2026-11-04, and the site said so in three languages.** "Coming soon", "Bientôt disponible" and "Em breve" sit together on the coming-soon page, and the body line ends "The doors open soon." That page gives no opening date, and we are not going to guess one.
- **On 2026-11-04, every other address it listed sent a visitor without a preview code back to the same door.** Its sitemap.xml listed fourteen URLs — the home page, /events, /blog, /cast, and named event and blog-post addresses such as kigali-night-strings, toronto-afrobeats-orchestra and welcome-to-afrozoneflix. BravData requested six of them — /events, /blog, /cast and three of the deeper pages — and each answered 302 to /coming-soon?redirect=… for a visitor without a code. The site also tells you exactly what a code does: it "opens the whole site on this browser for 30 days". Ten days later those same addresses served their own pages — see above.
- **It names seven cities, and states two rules of its own about showtime and tickets.** Lagos, London, Atlanta, Toronto, Nairobi, Accra and Houston — and next to them the site states that "Every showtime is announced in its own timezone" and that "Tickets are bought with a card." Those are its words, not our summary of a plan we have seen.
- **Its robots.txt reserves AI training while allowing search.** The file allows ordinary crawlers and declares "Content-Signal: search=yes,ai-train=no,use=reference", and it disallows several named training crawlers, Amazonbot and Applebot-Extended among them. That is the site's own published rule, quoted here as it is written.
