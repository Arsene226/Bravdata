A scanner is not clever. It reads what a browser receives, in the order it arrives, and it does not know anything you have not written down. Reading your own site the same way is a useful habit, because it separates the things you can fix this week from the things somebody wants to sell you.

## What a scanner can read

- The page title — the line in the browser tab, and often the line in a search result.
- The headings and paragraphs: what the page actually says, in words.
- The links on the page, and where they go.
- Image descriptions, if you have written them.
- Machine-readable details a site can publish about a local business: name, address, phone, opening hours.
- How quickly the page responds, and whether it works on a phone.
- Public signals elsewhere: pages that link to you or list you.

## What it cannot read

- Phone calls, walk-ins, or anything a customer said without typing it.
- Whether a person liked the page, or trusted it.
- Anything behind a login — your booking system, your inbox, your dashboard — unless you deliberately connect it.

That last point is why an honest report separates what was observed, what came from a connected account, and what was unavailable. A source we cannot read is printed as not checked. It is never turned into a zero, because zero is a number, and a number we cannot evidence would be invented.

## Read your own home page in this order

1. **Title.** If a customer saw only this line, would they know what you do and where?
2. **First heading.** One heading, saying the thing plainly. If the heading is your slogan, say it somewhere nearby.
3. **The first screen on a phone.** Is the service named, the phone number tappable, the area stated, the hours visible?
4. **The first hundred words.** The plainest sentence on the page should describe who you help and with what.
5. **Links.** Is there a page per service? Is contact reachable in one tap?
6. **Image descriptions.** Every photo needs a sentence describing it — for people who cannot see it, and for anything reading the page.
7. **Speed and phone-friendliness.** A page that takes several seconds to become readable is losing readers who never tell you.

## The finding that surprises owners

Many sites never actually say what the business does. The home page welcomes visitors and mentions being trusted, family-run or established, and the actual service lives three clicks in, on a page titled with an internal term nobody searches for.

If you read your site in the order above, that gap becomes obvious in about five minutes — which is usually cheaper than discovering it after paying for a rebuild you may not have needed.

## Write the date down

Whatever you find, record the day you read it. A finding without a date cannot be compared with anything later, and a before-and-after you cannot date is not evidence.
