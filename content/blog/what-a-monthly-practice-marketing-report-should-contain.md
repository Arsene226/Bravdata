A monthly report is the one document where you can check whether you are getting what you pay for. Most reports fail that test not by lying but by leaving things out: no source, no date, no blank where the data never arrived, and a summary that describes effort instead of evidence.

This is the structure we print, and the questions that let you judge any vendor's version of it — including ours.

## The sections, and why they are numbered

Our report runs from section 0 to section 8, appendix last, and the numbers do not move between months. Ask about "section 5" in March and it is the same section in June.

- **Report card.** The location, the days covered, the sources used and when each was obtained, who prepared and who reviewed the report, and the date of this cycle's call. A late report says so, and by how many business days.
- **What we changed this period.** Each completed task with who approved it, the date, the credits used, and the evidence — the live page, or the before-and-after observation. If nothing was delivered, it says so.
- **The numbers.** One row per metric: a one-sentence definition, this period, the previous period, the source, and a quality status — observed on a public page, reported by you and not verified, or not measured.
- **Visibility and reputation observations.** Listing fields as they appeared on a stated date, review count and rating with their source, new reviews and replies, and any AI-search answers quoted with their dates.
- **Inquiry and access.** Inquiries by channel and what the patient said, unanswered, bookings, no-shows, spend. Growth only, except where a Visibility client supplies the data.
- **What we could not measure, and why.** Always present: the metric, the reason, whose action unblocks it.
- **Data quality and definition notes.** Broken tracking, gaps in the month, any changed definition and the date it took effect.
- **What we recommend next.** Up to three items, each with the evidence behind it, a credit estimate, and whether it needs your approval.
- **Decisions needed from you**, with an owner and a date — then an **appendix** with every metric's definition and where each number came from.

## What a missing number prints as

One fixed sentence, every time:

> Not reported — nothing has been estimated and nothing from another period has been substituted.

Never a zero, never a bare dash, never last month's figure moved along. A real zero is different: a month with no new reviews prints zero, in words, because zero is something we measured. [What "not measurable yet" means](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/what-not-measurable-yet-means) is the longer argument for that rule.

## Three dates and one name

The report lands within five business days of the period closing; the 30-minute strategy call is held within seven, and it is not a sales call. One person is named as preparing the report, another as reviewing it. AI-search observations are one fixed five-question sample per location per cycle, quoted beside the date they were given — never scored, never a percentage, never compared month to month.

## How to judge any vendor's report

Ask for the last report rather than a description of it, then ask:

1. What does a missing number print as? Show me one.
2. Does every figure carry a source and the date it was observed?
3. Which numbers here did you not measure yourself?
4. What did you change last month, and what evidence shows it changed?
5. If you delivered nothing in a month, what would the report say?
6. What are you recommending, on what evidence, and what does it cost me?
7. Did someone other than the author check the figures?

What should worry you: a chart with no source, a percentage of visibility with no method, "growth" with no denominator, a monthly email that is a list of tasks with no evidence beside them. If the report is honest but the work is thin, that is a different conversation — [how to tell whether your marketing agency is doing anything](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/how-to-tell-whether-your-marketing-agency-is-doing-anything) covers it.

## What we do not put in it

No benchmark or industry average, because we do not have one and will not invent one. No composite score, index or "out of 10". No figure carried forward from another month to fill a gap.

Our own position, plainly: this structure is specified and built, and no client has received one of these reports yet.

## What this structure cannot tell you

- Whether the numbers present are correct. Only the source and the date let you check that.
- Whether the work was good. An honest blank makes a report trustworthy; it does not make the work effective.
- Whether next month will be better. A report describes what happened; nothing in it predicts what will.
