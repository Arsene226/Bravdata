A patient looking for primary care does not start by reading your website. They start by answering one question: is this the right practice for me, and can I act on it today? Six pieces of public information carry that answer, and they are the six most likely to disagree with each other, because each one lives somewhere different — a listing, a site, a booking tool, an insurance directory.

This is a checklist for a primary care practice in San Antonio, and it makes no claim about the San Antonio market: we hold no local data on how patients here choose a practice, or how any two compare. Everything below you can check yourself this week with a phone.

## 1. The name

- Write the name as it appears on the door, then compare it with the listing, the website, the booking page and the insurance directory — word for word, including "PA", "MD" or "and Associates".
- If the practice trades under a shorter name than its legal entity, say so in one line. A patient holding an insurance card with one version and a sign with another needs to know they are the same practice.

## 2. Hours

- Today's hours, the same in all four places. The usual failure is not a missing line but a line true in one place and stale in another.
- Separate clinic hours from phone hours, publish holiday hours before the holiday, and say which time zone the hours are in — hours are local time, Central in San Antonio, and a link gets forwarded.
- Check what the "open now" badge reads: the listing's hours, not your website's.

## 3. Location

- Street address with the suite number — the most commonly missing line, and the one a patient needs at the door.
- Which entrance, which floor, whether it is inside a larger building, and where to park. Check the directions link lands at the suite.
- With two locations, keep each page's address, hours and phone to that location only. The expensive version of this mistake is a patient booking at the wrong site.

## 4. Insurance and payment

- Name the plans you accept, plainly, with the date the list was last checked. An insurance list without a date is an undated claim about today.
- Say what to do when a plan is not listed, and state the ones you do not accept. "We do not take this plan" saves a call; silence costs one.

## 5. Services

- List the visit types a patient asks for: annual physical, same-day sick visit, chronic condition follow-up, vaccines, labs, referrals. Say whether you offer telehealth.
- State the ages you see. A practice that sees adults only should say so where a parent will read it.
- Use the words patients use on the phone, not only the professional name. If the patient's phrase appears nowhere on your page, that is a wording job, not a design one.

## 6. What the practice says it treats

- Say what the practice manages and what it refers out. The second half is the part almost nobody publishes, and it prevents the wrong booking.
- Keep it a scope statement. "We treat high blood pressure" describes the work; "we will get your blood pressure under control" is a claim about an outcome, and outcome language belongs to a clinical reviewer, not to whoever edits the site.

## Where the city name belongs

The city is a fact to state, not a market to describe: the location line, the hours' time zone, the practice name if it carries the city. It needs no paragraph about the neighbourhood, and no claim about local patients or local competition.

## What this checklist cannot tell you

It cannot tell you where you appear in search, how many patients you gained or lost, or how you compare with another practice. Nobody can read a number of missed patients off a listing, including us. What you can do is make the six fields agree, and date the check.

## Do it in one pass

Open your listing, your website and your booking page on a phone, logged out. Read each field out loud from all three, note every disagreement, then fix them in the order above.
