A website rebuild is usually the largest single line item a local business is offered. Sometimes it is the right answer. Often it is sold before anyone has written down what is actually wrong.

This is the list to work through before you pay for one.

## Name the problem first

"The site is old" is not a problem a customer has. "I cannot find the phone number on my phone" is. Write the problem in one sentence, in the customer's words, before you price anything.

If you cannot write that sentence, you are not ready to buy a website. You are ready to look at your own site.

## Signs a rebuild is probably not the answer

- Hours, phone and services disagree between your site, your listing and your booking page.
- The phone number is not tappable on a phone.
- One page describes five services, and no page describes any of them properly.
- The words customers use for your work appear nowhere on the page that sells it.
- The images are large enough that the page takes a while to become readable on mobile data.
- The booking link is below three screens of text.
- Nothing on the page tells a machine where you are and when you are open.

These are edits. Most of them are edits to text, and they can be done without touching the design.

## Signs the honest answer is a rebuild

- The platform can no longer be updated, and nobody can tell you whether it is safe.
- The site does not work on a phone and cannot be made to.
- Nobody can get access to the domain, the hosting or the site itself.
- Adding a page breaks the layout, so the structure can only shrink.
- The business has changed what it sells, and the site's structure still describes the old business.

## What a fix can do, and what it cannot

A fix can make the page readable, consistent and specific. It can put the customer's words on the page that sells the work, make the phone tappable, and make the details machine-readable.

A fix cannot make an inaccessible platform accessible. It cannot make a site nobody can edit editable, and it cannot give an old structure a page for every service without something breaking.

## The decision list

1. Write the customer's problem in one sentence.
2. Compare hours, phone and services across your site, your listing and your booking page.
3. Check whether each service you sell has a page written in the customer's words.
4. Check whether you can edit the site yourself, today, without calling anyone.
5. Check whether you hold the domain and hosting logins.
6. Get the fix and the rebuild priced separately, and ask in writing what the rebuild does that the fix cannot.

If the answer to the last question is "it will look better", that may still be worth paying for. But it is a design decision, not a visibility one, and you should know which you are buying.

## No promise about the rebuild

A rebuild changes what is published. It does not change who searches, and nobody can promise that a new site will bring more calls. We are not going to pretend otherwise, and neither should a proposal you are handed.

## What this list cannot tell you

- Whether your current platform is secure. Ask whoever maintains it, in writing.
- What a rebuild will cost. That is a quote, and it should be itemised.
- Whether a new site will produce more enquiries. A page can be easier to read and still not answer what people are looking for.

If you want to check the fixable things yourself first, [how to read your own website the way a scanner does](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/how-to-read-your-own-website-like-a-scanner) walks through what a machine can and cannot read on your pages.
