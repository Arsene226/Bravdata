A customer looking you up on a phone is not reading your website. They are trying to call you, book, or find where you are. A page that takes a while to become readable — or that makes the phone number a fingertip-sized puzzle — loses them before they reach the part where you can help.

That is not a statistic. It is what you see when you hand your phone to someone who has never used your site.

## Test it on your own phone first

- Open the site on mobile data, not the office wifi. Your customers are not on your wifi.
- Note how long until you can read the headline, and whether the photos arrive after the words.
- Tap the phone number. Does it dial?
- Find the address and the hours without pinching to zoom.
- Send yourself a message through the contact form, or start the booking flow and see where it stops you.
- Write down what happened, and the date.

A few lines of notes are enough: you are meeting your site the way a customer with a slow connection does.

## Check the speed with a free tool

Google PageSpeed Insights is a free, public tool: give it a page address and it reports load timings and what it found slow. Your browser's own developer tools do the same job in more detail, with a network panel that lists each file, its size and how long it took. Both are references, not a verdict on your business.

Run it twice — one run is noisy — and test the page that sells the work people call about, not just the home page.

## What to fix first

- **Oversized images.** A photo taken on a phone and uploaded at full size is usually far larger than the space it fills. Resize before upload; some site builders do it for you, so check.
- **Text and targets too small.** Body text you have to pinch to read, and links so close together you tap the wrong one.
- **A phone number that is not tappable.** A number inside a photo cannot be tapped, and cannot be read by a screen reader either.
- **The action below the fold.** If calling or booking is three screens down, it is not on the page as far as a customer in a hurry is concerned.
- **Anything that loads before the words.** A chat widget, a video, a slider of large photos — each makes the page feel slow before it says what you do.

None of these needs a redesign. Most are an edit to a file or a setting.

## What this cannot tell you

We will not promise you a search position, and we will not quote you extra calls. Order in search results comes from systems that change without notice, and how many customers you gained is a fact only you hold.

A faster, easier page is a better experience for the person already trying to reach you. Whether it is better is their judgement, not a figure we can print.

## Write it down

Record what you found and the day you found it. A finding without a date cannot be compared with anything later.
