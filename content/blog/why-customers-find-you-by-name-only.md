An owner types their business name into a phone, sees themselves at the top, and concludes that everything is fine. Then they type the thing they actually sell, plus their town, and find nothing. Both results are real, and they answer two different questions.

## Two different questions

A name search asks *is this the business I already know about?* Your name is written on your website, your listing, your invoices, your van and your social pages, so there is a lot of agreement for a search product to match against.

A service search asks *who does this near me?* There is no name to match. The only thing that can be matched is the words — the words the customer typed, against the words on pages that a search product can read.

## The words have to exist somewhere

A search product can only work with what is written down. That means your page titles, your headings, the first paragraphs of your pages, the service list on your listing, and the plain sentences that describe what you do.

If a customer's words never appear in any of those places, there is nothing to match. That is a gap in wording, not a technical failure.

## The ten-minute test

1. Write down three phrases customers actually used on the phone or at the counter this month. Their words, not yours.
2. Search each phrase, logged out, on mobile data, with your town or neighbourhood included. Note what appears and whether you do.
3. For each phrase, look for those exact words anywhere you control: the service list on your listing, a page on your site, a photo description, a booking page.
4. Be honest with the result. The most common finding is that the words appear nowhere at all.

## Why it happens

It is rarely a penalty and never a conspiracy. Internally you call the work by its professional name. Your customers call it by the problem they have. Your pages were usually written once, years ago, by someone who knew the business well — which is exactly why the customer's version of the words never made it in.

Nothing is broken. The question and the answer simply do not use the same vocabulary.

## What to do, in order

1. Fix the plain fields first: hours, phone number and services, so nothing contradicts itself. We wrote a short method for that here: [check your hours, phone and services first](https://www.bravdata.io/blog/hours-phone-and-services-first).
2. Name each service in the customer's words on the page that sells it. One service, one page, written plainly.
3. Say where you serve, in words — the town, the neighbourhood, the area you actually cover.
4. Keep it true. Padding a page with every place name in the state to cover more searches is the kind of thing customers notice and search products discount.

## What this test cannot tell you

- Your ranking. Nobody can read a position off a search page: results vary by person, place and day, so we do not print one and neither should anyone else.
- Whether appearing more often means more customers. That is a marketing promise, and it is not one we will make on anyone's behalf.
- Why a particular competitor appeared. You can see what they publish; you cannot see their numbers.

What the test does give you is something you can check: whether the words your customers use exist on the pages you control, and whether they are true.
