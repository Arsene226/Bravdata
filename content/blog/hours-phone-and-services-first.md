When someone finds you, they are rarely reading for pleasure. They are checking whether they can do something right now: call, book, or turn up. Three fields carry that decision, and they are the three most likely to disagree with each other.

## Hours

The trap is not a missing line. It is a line that is true in one place and wrong in another: summer hours on the website, winter hours on the listing, holiday closures in a post nobody will scroll to.

Check it this way:

- Open your listing, your website and your booking page on a phone, logged out.
- Read the hours out loud. If you stumble, a customer will too.
- Confirm the awkward cases: the one day a week you close early, the lunch hour, and what happens on a public holiday.
- Check that wherever the hours are shown, they say what time zone or area they mean.

## Phone number

A phone number fails in three ways: it dials somewhere nobody is, it dials nowhere, or it cannot be tapped.

- Tap the number on your listing and on your website from your own phone. Does it dial?
- Where does it ring? Is that the desk someone actually sits at?
- If you use a tracking number anywhere, check the customer-facing number is the same everywhere a customer might look.
- Confirm the number is text on the page, not part of an image. A number inside a picture cannot be tapped, and cannot be read by assistive software either.

## Services

This is the field most often written in the wrong language. Internally you might call the work by its professional name; customers call it by the problem.

- Write down the five jobs you are asked for most often, in the words the customer used on the phone.
- Compare those words with your listing's service list and your website.
- If the customer's words nowhere appear in either place, that is a gap worth closing, and it is a wording job, not a design job.

## Why these three

They are the fields a customer needs in order to act, and they are the ones most likely to be duplicated across surfaces: a website someone updates yearly, a listing nobody has opened since setup, and a booking tool with its own settings page. Every duplicate is a chance for two answers to exist.

Fixing a disagreement usually takes minutes. Nobody has to approve a new brand for you to make your Tuesday hours agree with each other.

## What you are not doing

You are not chasing a ranking, redesigning anything, or buying a tool. And no honest audit can tell you how many customers a wrong number cost you — nobody can read that off a listing, including us. What you can do is remove the contradiction before the next customer meets it.

## Keep it that way

Note who can change each field and how they get in: your website (whoever administers it), your listing (whoever verified it), your booking tool (its own admin). Keep one list with the person who holds those logins, and check the three fields on a schedule, not after a complaint.
